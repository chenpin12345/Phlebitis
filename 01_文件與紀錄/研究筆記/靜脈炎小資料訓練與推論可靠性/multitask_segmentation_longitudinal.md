## (A) Multi-task / multimodal learning: shared-backbone MTL, detect→crop→classify, ordinal staging, and clinical-metadata fusion

### Takeaway
With 14 patients, a single shared-backbone network that jointly does detection + binary + staging has no supporting evidence at this scale and a real negative-transfer risk; a simple two-stage pipeline (existing YOLO detector → human-confirmed box → dilated lesion crop → frozen-feature classifier with ordinal-aware head or ordinal logistic model) is cheaper, auditable and better aligned with the human-confirmation principle. Clinical metadata (dwell time, drug class, site) has solid epidemiological backing as phlebitis risk factors, but in image+metadata fusion studies the gain is modest (≈1–7 points balanced accuracy) and can vanish or reverse on a test set because of missingness/shortcut effects — so NOW it should be collected as structured fields and analysed separately, not fused.

### Cited Findings

#### A1. Multi-task learning (MTL): benefits and negative transfer
- **RE** Hard parameter sharing (shared hidden layers + task-specific heads, going back to Caruana 1993) is the most common deep MTL design and is argued to reduce overfitting risk; Ruder cites Baxter (1997) that the overfitting risk of shared parameters is of order N (number of tasks) smaller. Note this is a cited theoretical bound, not an empirical medical result. — [Ruder S. 2017, An Overview of Multi-Task Learning in Deep Neural Networks, arXiv:1706.05098 [D]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.05098) (accessed 2026-10-31)
- **RE (counter-evidence)** Standley et al. (ICML 2020) show that competing tasks trained in one network can degrade each other's performance; they propose grouping cooperating tasks and splitting competing tasks into separate networks, and found that transfer-learning affinity is a weak predictor of multi-task affinity (i.e., you cannot assume tasks help each other just because they are related). — [Standley T, Zamir A, Chen D, Guibas L, Malik J, Savarese S. Which Tasks Should Be Learned Together in Multi-task Learning? ICML 2020, PMLR 119:9120–9132 [B]](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/standley20a.html) (accessed 2026-10-31). Evidence is from Taskonomy-scale natural-image data, not small medical data.
- **RE** Kendall, Gal & Cipolla (CVPR 2018): MTL performance "is strongly dependent on the relative weighting between each task's loss"; they learn the weights via homoscedastic (task) uncertainty, and the joint model outperformed separately trained models on depth regression + semantic + instance segmentation (Cityscapes-scale data). DOI 10.1109/CVPR.2018.00781. — [Kendall A, Gal Y, Cipolla R. CVPR 2018, pp. 7482–7491 [B]](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2018/html/Kendall_Multi-Task_Learning_Using_CVPR_2018_paper.html) (accessed 2026-10-31)
- **RE** A medical MTL review (Zhao et al. 2023) groups medical multi-task deep networks into cascaded, parallel, interacted and hybrid architectures across eight body-region application areas and notes persistent performance gaps on some tasks. DOI 10.1016/j.compbiomed.2022.106496. — [Zhao Y, Wang X, Che T, Bao G, Li S. Multi-task deep learning for medical image computing and analysis: A review. Comput Biol Med 2023;153:106496 [C]](https://guoqingbao.github.io/publication/2023-02-15-MTL-Medical) (author page; accessed 2026-10-31). Full text not read; no small-data quantitative synthesis was retrieved.
- **RE (skin, indirect)** Skin-lesion MTL papers (segmentation-as-attention + classifier with weighted joint loss) motivate MTL explicitly as an anti-overfitting device for small labelled sets, but the retrieved abstracts do not report a controlled negative-transfer analysis. — [Multi-task Explainable Skin Lesion Classification, arXiv:2310.07209 [D]](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.07209) (accessed 2026-10-31). Leakage/external validation: not verified from abstract.
- 未找到可靠證據: no study was found that trains detection + binary + 3-level staging jointly on a dataset of ~100 photos / ~14 patients, with patient-level CV, and reports benefit or harm.

#### A2. Does Ultralytics support a multi-head (detect + classify) model?
- **IP** Not officially. A YOLOv8 feature request for a generalized multi-task model (DET/OBB/SEG/POSE) received a positive maintainer reply but was marked stale. — [ultralytics/ultralytics issue #12174 [E]](https://github.com/ultralytics/ultralytics/issues/12174) (accessed 2026-10-31)
- **IP** In YOLOv5, a maintainer stated the classification branch trains a standalone classifier and does not merge tasks. — [ultralytics/yolov5 issue #13086 [E]](https://github.com/ultralytics/yolov5/issues/13086) (accessed 2026-10-31)
- **IP** A YOLO26 community thread asks how to build shared backbone/neck with separate heads — i.e., requires custom architecture work. — [Ultralytics community: Extending YOLO26 for custom multi-task architecture [E]](https://community.ultralytics.com/t/extending-yolo26-for-custom-multi-task-architecture/1811) (accessed 2026-10-31)
- **IP** A third-party research fork modifies YOLOv8 for joint detection + segmentation heads (not classification/staging, not maintained by Ultralytics). — [jiayuanwang-jw/yolov8-multi-task [E]](https://github.com/jiayuanwang-jw/yolov8-multi-task) (accessed 2026-10-31)
- Simpler alternative available in the current stack: YOLO detection classes could encode stage (e.g., classes "stage1_lesion", "stage2_lesion"), which is a de facto detect+stage model inside one Ultralytics `detect` task (**INF**; with only ~24 boxed photos this splits an already tiny box set across classes and is not recommended NOW).

#### A3. Lesion-crop vs whole-image classification
- **RE** Mahbod et al. (ISIC 2017 dermoscopy): using segmentation masks did not significantly improve melanoma classification in any scenario; dilated (context-preserving) cropping significantly improved seborrheic-keratosis classification; removing all background **significantly degraded** performance; manual vs automatic masks made no significant difference for dilated cropping. DOI 10.1016/j.cmpb.2020.105725. — [Mahbod A, Tschandl P, Langs G, Ecker R, Ellinger I. The effects of skin lesion segmentation on the performance of dermatoscopic image classification. Comput Methods Programs Biomed 2020 [C]; arXiv:2008.12602](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.12602) (accessed 2026-10-31). Leakage: ISIC 2017 is lesion-level, patient IDs not controlled; no external validation.
- **RE (shortcut evidence supporting cropping)** Winkler et al. (JAMA Dermatol 2019): a CE-marked CNN's specificity on 130 melanocytic lesions fell from 84.1% (unmarked) to 45.8% (with surgical skin-marker ink); cropping the markings out restored specificity to 97.2% (AUC 0.993). Authors suggest the CNN associated ink with melanoma. — [Winkler JK et al. JAMA Dermatol 2019;155:1135–1141, PubMed 31411641 [B]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31411641/); [JAMA full text](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamadermatology/fullarticle/2740808) (accessed 2026-10-31). Relevance: IV-site photos contain tape, dressings, catheter hubs, labels, pen marks, bed sheets — whole-image models can learn these.
- **RE (mixed)** Search-snippet-level reports (not verified from full text): Nunnari et al. recommend cropping to reduce artifact learning; a 2026 arXiv paper argues tight crops can still carry non-diagnostic background cues. — [DFKI KI-2021 skin lesion masking paper [C/E]](https://dfki.de/fileadmin/user_upload/import/11859_2021_KIconference_SkinLesionMasking.pdf); [arXiv:2603.12445 [D]](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.12445) (accessed 2026-10-31)
- 未找到可靠證據: no head-to-head crop-vs-whole-image comparison on **clinical (non-dermoscopic) smartphone photos** of small datasets was found.

#### A4. Ordinal staging (normal < stage 1 < stage 2)
- **RE** CORAL (Cao, Mirjalili, Raschka): converts K ordinal classes into K−1 binary tasks with shared weights and guarantees rank-monotonic (consistent) outputs; architecture-agnostic; largest reported gains were on face age estimation. — [Cao W, Mirjalili V, Raschka S. Rank consistent ordinal regression for neural networks with application to age estimation. Pattern Recognit Lett 2020;140:325–331 [B]; arXiv:1901.07884](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.07884) (accessed 2026-10-31)
- **RE** CORN relaxes CORAL's weight-sharing constraint by training on conditional subsets and chaining conditional probabilities; code and splits public. DOI 10.1007/s10044-023-01181-9. — [Shi X, Cao W, Raschka S. Pattern Anal Appl 2023;26(3):941–955 [C]; arXiv:2111.08851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.08851) (accessed 2026-10-31)
- **RE (counter-evidence / alternatives)** Snippet-level: an ulcerative-colitis endoscopic grading study found class-distance-weighted cross-entropy outperformed categorical CE and CORN; a 2026 preprint (Ordinal Cross-Entropy) reports better calibration than CORAL/CORN on benchmarks including diabetic retinopathy. Neither was read in full. — [arXiv:2606.25769 [D]](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.25769) (accessed 2026-10-31)
- 未找到可靠證據: no controlled comparison of CORAL / CORN / plain CE / cumulative-link (proportional-odds) models on a medical dataset with <50 patients was found. With 3 classes there are only 2 thresholds; a proportional-odds logistic regression on frozen DINOv2 features (e.g., `mord` or `statsmodels` OrderedModel) is the lowest-parameter option (**INF**).

#### A5. Clinical (non-image) risk factors for PIVC phlebitis — evidence base for metadata fields
- **RE** Lv & Zhang meta-analysis (35 studies): pooled phlebitis incidence 30.7 per 100 catheters (95% CI 27.2–34.2); severe phlebitis 3.6% (2.7–4.6); associated factors: longer dwell time, antibiotic infusion, female sex (OR 1.42), forearm insertion, infectious disease, Teflon catheters. — [Lv L, Zhang J. The incidence and risk of infusion phlebitis with peripheral intravenous catheters: a meta-analysis. J Vasc Access 2020;21(3):342–349 [B]](https://vufind-test.katalog.k.utb.cz/SummonRecord/FETCH-LOGICAL-c337t-dec754c68dcfd3fdec7ef10a5c713282955fedb5749e125da760032881c5cc902) (catalogue record; numbers from abstract via search; accessed 2026-10-31)
- **RE** Marsh et al. systematic review/meta-analysis: 103 studies (70 observational, 33 RCTs), 76,977 PIVCs; phlebitis (with definition) 19.3%; phlebitis and infiltration are the commonest failure reasons. DOI 10.1111/jan.14565. — [Marsh N et al. Peripheral intravenous catheter non-infectious complications in adults: a systematic review and meta-analysis. J Adv Nurs 2020 [B]](https://zendy.io/title/10.1111/jan.14565) (accessed 2026-10-31; numbers from search snippet)
- **RE** Ethiopian pooled analysis (2024): incidence 41/100 catheters; joint-area insertion AOR 2.44, longer dwell AOR 3.96, drug + blood in same vein AOR 1.91. — [Springer Discover-series article, doi 10.1007/s44337-024-00113-8 [C]](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44337-024-00113-8) (accessed 2026-10-31; snippet-level)
- **RE (contradiction on gauge/site)** A 2018 meta-analysis (14 studies) found ≤20G vs larger gauge RR 0.88 (n.s.) and antecubital vs other upper-limb RR 1.05 (n.s.), despite background claims that gauge and site matter. — [Occurrence of Phlebitis: A Systematic Review and Meta-analysis, PubMed 29698331 [B]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29698331/) (accessed 2026-10-31; snippet-level)
- **RE (dwell time / replacement policy)** Cochrane review: no clear difference in thrombophlebitis between clinically-indicated and routine replacement (RR 1.07, 95% CI 0.93–1.25; moderate certainty). DOI 10.1002/14651858.CD007798.pub5. — [Webster J, Osborne S, Rickard CM, Marsh N. Cochrane Database Syst Rev 2019 [B]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30671926/) (accessed 2026-10-31). Implication: "dwell time" relates to risk in observational data, but routine scheduled replacement does not reduce phlebitis — dwell time is confounded by care policy.
- **RE (drugs)** Flebitis Zero nested case-control within a prospective cohort (93 Spanish hospitals, 9,409 patients, 13,820 PIVCs): highest adjusted ORs caspofungin aOR 10.81 (2.89–40.49), ganciclovir aOR 8.14 (1.63–40.55), amiodarone aOR 4.14 (2.40–7.16); potassium-containing infusions 8.3% vs 4.1% (unadjusted, p=0.0006). — [Pharmacological burden and chemical phlebitis associated with peripheral venous catheters: nested case-control, Flebitis Zero project. Heart & Lung 2026;79:102836, doi 10.1016/j.hrtlng.2026.102836 [C]](https://portalinvestigacion.uniovi.es/documentos/6a1b7884e3ade41446333b19) (repository record; accessed 2026-10-31; full text not read). Vancomycin: only a snippet-level HR 1.73 with unclear outcome — 未找到可靠證據 for a vancomycin-specific phlebitis estimate.
- **RE** Amiodarone (AJCC 2013, n=105): phlebitis ~40%; total peripheral dose, infusion duration and number of catheters associated. — [Norton et al. Am J Crit Care 2013, PubMed 24186821 [C]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24186821) (accessed 2026-10-31; snippet-level)
- **A (standards)** INS Infusion Therapy Standards of Practice, 9th ed. J Infus Nurs 2024;47(1S Suppl 1):S1–S285, doi 10.1097/NAN.0000000000000532; an updated non-antineoplastic vesicant list was published in J Infus Nurs Sept/Oct 2024. Exact INS phlebitis-scale wording and vesicant definition were **not** retrieved (paywalled). — [Wolters Kluwer product page for INS Standards 2024 [A]](https://www.wolterskluwer.com/en/solutions/ovid/infusion-therapy-standards-of-practice-2024-9th-edition-16655) (accessed 2026-10-31)
- **RE (label instruments)** Ray-Barruel et al. systematic review: 71 phlebitis scales; only VIP, INS and PVC ASSESS had any psychometric testing and "none has been thoroughly validated"; INS scale inter-rater kappa 0.45; PVC ASSESS erythema kappa 0.40 in person and 0.58 from photographs; VIP (Jackson) items: pain, erythema, swelling, induration, palpable venous cord, pyrexia; INS (2006) items include streak formation and palpable cord >2.54 cm; 23% of 233 studies gave no phlebitis definition. DOI 10.1111/jep.12107. — [Ray-Barruel G, Polit DF, Murfield JE, Rickard CM. Infusion phlebitis assessment measures: a systematic review. #### A6. Image + metadata fusion evidence (dermatology)
- **RE (positive)** Pacheco & Krohling 2020 (PAD-UFES-20, smartphone clinical images + patient data): combining images with clinical info gave "a general improvement of around 7%" balanced accuracy across models. DOI 10.1016/j.compbiomed.2019.103545. — [Pacheco AGC, Krohling RA. The impact of patient clinical information on automated skin cancer detection. Comput Biol Med 2020;116:103545 [C]; arXiv:1909.12912](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.12912) (accessed 2026-10-31). Leakage: PAD-UFES-20 has multiple images per patient; patient-level split not verified from the abstract; no external validation.
- **RE (positive, modest)** Pacheco & Krohling 2021 MetaBlock (attention-style metadata gating): improved all tested models on two skin datasets and beat concatenation/MetaNet in 6 of 10 scenarios (6/10 from an aggregator abstract). DOI 10.1109/JBHI.2021.3062002. — [IEEE JBHI 2021;25(9):3554–3563 [B]](https://bvbvf1.bib-bvb.de/vufind/PrimoRecord/cdi_proquest_journals_2568779719) (catalogue record; accessed 2026-10-31)
- **RE (counter-evidence — key)** Gessert et al., ISIC 2019 winner: metadata (age, sex, site) improved cross-validated mean sensitivity by ~1–2 points (e.g., ensemble 72.5→74.2), mostly for smaller models; but on the **official test set** the metadata task scored **63.4 vs 63.6** for image-only. Authors attribute this to the "unknown" class whose training images had no metadata (AUC 0.775→0.612) — i.e., the model overfit to metadata missingness. DOI 10.1016/j.mex.2020.100864. — [Gessert N et al. Skin lesion classification using ensembles of multi-resolution EfficientNets with meta data. MethodsX 2020;7:100864 [C]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7150512) (accessed 2026-10-31)
- **RE** A Lisbon thesis on ISIC 2019 fusion: +3.14 (val) / +3.79 (test) BACC points over no-metadata; a 2025 study (17 models, 3 fusion methods) reports +2.22 BACC on ISIC2019 and age as most impactful. — [Almeida L., IST Lisbon dissertation [E]](https://fenix.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/downloadFile/1126295043837176/Extended_Abstract_LeandroAlmeida_84112.pdf) (accessed 2026-10-31; thesis = not peer-reviewed)

#### A7. Data collection / privacy (brief, per instructions)
- **INF** Minimum structured fields justified by A5: insertion date-time (to compute dwell hours at photo time), site (hand dorsum / forearm / antecubital / other; left/right), catheter gauge and material if known, infusate classes (antibiotic Y/N; named high-risk drugs: amiodarone, KCl, vancomycin, caspofungin, ganciclovir; parenteral nutrition), same-vein blood products Y/N, patient age band (e.g., 10-year bands), sex. Use the INS or VIP scale item-by-item (redness, swelling, pain, cord length cm, purulence) rather than a single stage, so stage can be recomputed and inter-rater agreement measured per item (Ray-Barruel shows items-level kappa can be assessed from photographs).
- **INF** Taiwan Personal Data Protection Act (個人資料保護法): health/medical records are a special category; store age bands not birth dates, relative day-offsets not calendar dates, no names/chart numbers in filenames — legal detail out of scope here.

### Inferences
- **INF — NOW (120 photos / 14 patients):**
  - Do **not** build a shared-backbone detect+binary+stage network. Expected benefit ≈ 0 to negative: 14 patient groups → each StratifiedGroupKFold fold has ~4–5 test patients, so any MTL gain is far below the CI width; loss-weighting (Kendall) adds hyper-parameters that cannot be tuned honestly at this size. Standley 2020 shows tasks can compete; no small-data evidence counters this.
  - Recommended pipeline (fits stack, all human-in-the-loop): YOLO11n/s box → human confirms/edits box in UI → dilated crop (e.g., box expanded 1.5–2× to keep perilesional skin; Mahbod shows full background removal hurts) → frozen DINOv2-small / EfficientNet-B0 embedding → logistic regression (binary) and ordinal logistic / CORAL-style 2-threshold head (stage). Compare against current whole-image baseline under the same patient-level folds. Minimum data: what exists; only ~24 photos have boxes, so for the remaining ~96 photos crops must come from human-confirmed YOLO proposals (draft status until confirmed).
  - Ordinal: report both nominal and ordinal heads; add ordinal metrics (MAE between stage indices, quadratic-weighted kappa) alongside macro-F1. Note that binary (phlebitis yes/no) and stage share the "normal" class with only 9 photos — any normal-class estimate (false-alarm rate) has very wide Wilson CIs.
  - Metadata: collect, do not fuse. Analyse separately (descriptive tables; at most a 1–2-variable logistic model with explicit "exploratory" label). 14 patients cannot support a fusion model; an image+tabular model would mostly learn patient identity (each patient has many photos with near-identical metadata).
  - GPU: frozen-feature + sklearn pipeline runs on CPU or <2 GB VRAM; YOLO11n/s fine-tuning at 640 px batch 8–16 fits 8 GB (**INF**, no benchmark retrieved for this exact config).
- **INF — FUTURE (~1,000 photos, balanced three classes):**
  - Two-stage pipeline remains the baseline. A shared-backbone classifier with two heads (binary + ordinal stage) is reasonable to try because the tasks are nested (binary = stage>0), so a single ordinal head already yields the binary answer — this "MTL" is nearly free and avoids negative transfer. Joint detection+classification inside Ultralytics requires custom architecture (not officially supported) — not worth it unless detector and classifier are both stable.
  - Fusion: late fusion (separate image model probability + tabular logistic model, combined by stacking within patient-group CV) is the most auditable; MetaBlock-type gating is an option only with ≥ several hundred patients (**INF**; PAD-UFES-20 patient count not verified in this round).
  - Expected benefit of fusion (from dermatology analogues): +1–7 BACC points, with real risk of zero or negative on held-out data (Gessert).
- **INF — Shortcut / leakage risks specific to metadata:**
  - Dwell time and "hospital vs LINE source" are likely strongly associated with the label (hospital photos are often taken because phlebitis is suspected; LINE users may self-select) — the model could learn source/dwell rather than appearance. Mitigation: report image-only and metadata-only baselines; stratify CV by source; check metadata-only model performance — if metadata alone ≈ fusion, the image adds nothing.
  - Missingness shortcut (Gessert): LINE uploads will lack most clinical fields while hospital photos have them → missingness pattern = source = label proxy. Never fuse until missingness is balanced across classes; encode "unknown" explicitly and test a missingness-only model.
  - Hospital stage labels derived from filename rules: if filenames encode dates/sides correlated with stage, any metadata derived from filenames can leak the label.
- **INF — Conflicts with inviolable principles:** None for the recommended design. A shared multi-head model is not itself a conflict, but any automatic pseudo-labelling of stage from model output into the DB would be — keep all model outputs as "suggestion" records until a human confirms.

## (B) Segmentation & quantitative measurement of erythema/swelling (SAM / SAM 2 / MedSAM; colour, area, calibration)

### Takeaway
Box-prompted SAM-family models are a reasonable **annotation assistant** (draft masks a human corrects) but evidence shows unstable zero-shot performance on medical targets with ambiguous/low-contrast boundaries — exactly what diffuse erythema is — and no study evaluates SAM on phlebitis or diffuse erythema; quantitative redness/area numbers from uncalibrated LINE smartphone photos are not reliable, while calibrated photos (colour card + scale marker, perpendicular shot) can achieve wound-area ICCs ≈0.97–0.99 and substantially lower colour error. NOW: use SAM/MobileSAM only to speed up human mask drawing on the existing ~24 boxed photos and pilot a photo protocol; FUTURE: explainable features (calibrated a*, redness area in cm², streak length) feeding a simple classifier can be tested as an auxiliary to the image model.

### Cited Findings

#### B1. SAM family — capabilities and failure evidence
- **RE** Mazurowski et al. (19 medical datasets): SAM performs "notably better with box prompts than with point prompts"; single-prompt IoU ranged from 0.1135 (spine MRI) to 0.8650 (hip X-ray); better on well-circumscribed, less-ambiguous objects, poorer on e.g. brain tumours; with multiple iterative points, SAM improved only slightly while RITM/SimpleClick/FocalClick overtook it. DOI 10.1016/j.media.2023.102918. — [Mazurowski MA, Dong H, Gu H, Yang J, Konz N, Zhang Y. Segment anything model for medical image analysis: an experimental study. Med Image Anal 2023, article 102918 [B]; arXiv:2304.10517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.10517v3) (accessed 2026-10-31)
- **RE** Huang et al. (53 open datasets, 18 modalities, 84 object types, ~1.05M 2D images): box/point prompts beat "Everything" mode; zero-shot "is not sufficient" for direct medical use; performance unstable or totally failed for some objects, depending on boundary complexity and intensity differences; fine-tuning raised average Dice by 4.39% (ViT-B) and 6.68% (ViT-H). DOI 10.1016/j.media.2023.103061 (Southampton repository lists vol. 92; header year 2024). — [Huang Y et al. Segment anything model for medical images? Med Image Anal 2024;92:103061 [B]; arXiv:2304.14660](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14660v7) (accessed 2026-10-31)
- **RE** MedSAM: SAM fine-tuned on ~1.57M image–mask pairs across 10 modalities; evaluated on 86 internal and 60 external validation tasks; matched or exceeded specialist models in many cases; dermoscopy is listed among under-represented modalities. DOI 10.1038/s41467-024-44824-z. — [Ma J, He Y, et al. Segment anything in medical images. Nat Commun 2024;15:654 [B]](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-024-44824-z) (accessed 2026-10-31). MedSAM is box-prompted, trained on dermoscopy not clinical smartphone photos.
- **RE** SAM-Med2D: SAM encoder+decoder fine-tuned on SA-Med2D-20M (4.6M images, 19.7M masks). — [Cheng J et al. SAM-Med2D, arXiv:2308.16184 [D]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.16184v1) (accessed 2026-10-31)
- **RE (skin)** A 12-dataset benchmark incl. ISIC found SAM's Dice trailed medical-specific algorithms on every dataset by ~0.1–0.5 (sometimes 0.6–0.7) — snippet-level. — [arXiv:2304.09324 [D]](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2304.09324) (accessed 2026-10-31). A dermoscopy SAM study reported Dice ~0.65–0.66 with post-processing helping boundaries — snippet-level. — [Selçuk Univ. repository: Skin lesion segmentation with semantic SAM: pros and cons [C/E]](https://acikerisim.selcuk.edu.tr/items/6d99ec09-8065-4c91-b14c-3dd1d0ec6a8c/full) (accessed 2026-10-31)
- **RE (box robustness)** SAM-like models are sensitive to natural box-prompt noise (2026 preprint), so user-drawn or YOLO-predicted boxes of varying tightness will change masks. — [BREPS, arXiv:2601.15123 [D]](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2601.15123) (accessed 2026-10-31)
- 未找到可靠證據: no evaluation of SAM / SAM 2 / MedSAM on phlebitis, cellulitis-type diffuse erythema, or IV-site photos; no study of SAM on swelling (which is mostly a 3-D/shading cue, not a colour boundary).

#### B2. Tooling: Ultralytics and Meta SAM 2 — support, licence, speed

#### B4. Area / length measurement and calibration
- **RE** imitoMeasure (contactless digital planimetry) vs acetate tracing in spinal-cord-injury pressure ulcers: validity ICC 0.97 (95% CI 0.93–0.99), intra-rater ~0.99; minimal detectable change 17–35%; small sample. — [Do Khac A et al. mHealth app for pressure ulcer wound assessment. ### Inferences
- **INF — NOW (120 photos / 14 patients):**
  - SAM use = annotation assistant only. Wire: React UI shows YOLO box (or annotator box) → FastAPI endpoint calls Ultralytics `SAM("mobile_sam.pt" or "sam2.1_t.pt")` with `bboxes=` locally → returns polygon as a **draft** mask → annotator edits with positive/negative clicks or polygon tool → only "confirmed" masks are written to SQLite. Do **not** use `auto_annotate` to write label files directly — that conflicts with the "human-confirmed before written" rule; if used, redirect its output to a draft folder/table.
  - GPU: MobileSAM / SAM2.1-t inference should run in well under 8 GB VRAM (**INF** from parameter counts 10–39M; no official VRAM figure found); SAM2-b/l also plausibly fit for single-image inference at 1024 px but unverified. On Windows, Ultralytics wrappers avoid the SAM 2 repo's CUDA-extension/WSL issues.
  - Do not fine-tune SAM/MedSAM now (24 boxed photos; Huang shows fine-tuning gains require task data; risk of memorising 14 patients).
  - Do not report redness area in cm² or a* values as findings from current photos: no scale or colour reference in the images, LINE compression and unknown white balance. At most, compute **within-photo relative** features (e.g., lesion-region a* minus adjacent-normal-skin a*, both in the same photo) as exploratory features — relative contrast partly cancels global white-balance shifts (**INF**, not validated).
  - Pilot a photography protocol (see below) with nurses at E-Da on new photos only.
- **INF — FUTURE (~1,000 photos):**
  - With calibrated photos, explainable features (lesion-minus-reference Δa*, erythema area cm², longest streak/cord length cm on the skeleton of the mask, swelling via left-right comparison if both arms photographed) + logistic regression can be compared against the image model; Kim et al. 2020 is analogous evidence that hand-crafted features can beat deep features at ~100-patient scale.
  - Fine-tuning: SAM 2 training code exists (Apache-2.0) but Windows → WSL; Ultralytics wrappers cannot train SAM. Alternative fully inside Ultralytics: train YOLO11n-seg/YOLO26n-seg on the human-confirmed SAM-assisted masks (training supported; ~25 ms CPU inference). This is likely the most practical FUTURE route (**INF**).
  - Minimum data (INF): a segmentation model for diffuse erythema needs a few hundred confirmed masks from ≥50 patients for any credible patient-level evaluation; mask inter-rater agreement (Dice between two annotators on ~30 photos) should be measured first, because if humans agree only at Dice ~0.6 a model cannot be judged better than that.
- **INF — Practical photography protocol (for nurses and LINE users):**
  1. Place a small printed card (colour patches — white/grey/red-skin patches — plus an ArUco/QR marker or 2 cm scale bar) flat on the skin next to, not over, the IV site; same card design for all.
  2. Phone parallel to skin (perpendicular view), ~20–30 cm, whole forearm/hand dorsum in frame, catheter site centred.
  3. No flash or consistent flash policy; avoid direct sunlight/coloured lamps; neutral background.
  4. Photograph the contralateral limb at the same site if possible (within-patient normal reference for colour and swelling).
  5. Record time since insertion, site (LH/RH/LL/RL codes already used), and VIP/INS item scores at the same time.
  6. LINE: ask users to send as "original/file" rather than compressed image if LINE settings allow (**INF**; LINE compression behaviour not researched here).
  7. Remove/avoid pen marks, labels with identifiers, and faces; tape/dressing should be documented because it can act as a shortcut (Winkler 2019).
- **INF — Risks:** SAM masks on diffuse redness can be confidently wrong (spurious boundaries at tape edges, veins, shadows); colour features are skin-tone and device dependent; East Asian skin evidence is missing; area measurement on a curved forearm is only approximate (2-D planimetry error up to ~10% per snippet-level source); SAM-licence: SAM/SAM 2/MobileSAM are Apache-2.0 (permissive) — no conflict; Ultralytics itself is AGPL-3.0 (not checked in this round; verify for any distribution).

## (C) Longitudinal prediction from serial photos (worsening / improvement)

### Takeaway
Longitudinal change-detection evidence comes from datasets with hundreds to thousands of patients (e.g., Siamese networks on 870 and 3,021 patients; test sets of 65 and 272 patients), and even the closest wound-photo study used only the first-visit photo; with 14 patients the number of independent within-patient stage transitions is far too small to train or validate any change predictor, so NOW is limited to descriptive serial measurement and building a properly time-stamped dataset; FUTURE (still likely <100 patients with ≥2 visits) could support a frozen-feature pairwise "change vs no change" analysis as exploratory research, not prediction.

### Cited Findings
- **RE** Li et al. (npj Digit Med 2020): Siamese network (two ResNet subnetworks, contrastive loss) trained on image pairs labelled only "change/no change" produced continuous severity scores (Spearman ρ 0.87 ROP, 0.89 knee OA vs expert ranking). Longitudinal change test sets: ROP 123 comparisons from 65 patients; knee OA 521 comparisons from 272 patients, one comparison per patient; change-detection AUC 0.90 (95% CI 0.88–0.92) ROP pairwise and 0.88 (0.84–0.92) knee OA; kappa 0.41–0.66; patient-level 80/10/10 split with no overlap; **no external validation**; registration not described (vessel segmentation / joint cropping instead); full cohorts 4,861 images/870 patients and 10,012 images/3,021 patients. DOI 10.1038/s41746-020-0255-1. — [Li MD, Chang K, Bearce B, et al. Siamese neural networks for continuous disease severity evaluation and change detection in medical imaging. npj Digit Med 2020;3:48 [B]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7099081) (accessed 2026-10-31)
- **RE** Short-term dermoscopic lesion change detection with a Siamese network + segmentation regularisation used an in-house dataset of 1,000 lesion image pairs (short time-frame). — [Zhang et al. (first-author initials not verified). Short-term lesion change detection for melanoma screening with novel Siamese neural network. IEEE Trans Med Imaging 2021 [B]](https://ses.library.usyd.edu.au/handle/2123/29813) (repository record; accessed 2026-10-31; full text/metrics not verified — repository returned HTTP 403; patient count and validation design not verified)
- **RE** Spatio-temporal two-stream network on serial dermoscopy trained on 184 cases (preprint). — [Yu et al. Melanoma diagnosis with spatio-temporal feature learning on sequential dermoscopic images, arXiv:2006.10950 [D]](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2006.10950) (accessed 2026-10-31). Leakage/external validation not verified.
- **RE** Wound healing: Kim et al. 2020 (diabetic foot ulcers, 155 patients / 2,291 visits available, 113 patients / 208 wounds analysed) used **only the initial-visit** image to predict eventual healing (best test AUROC 0.794, CV ~0.69); authors list not using follow-up images as a limitation; only 208 of ~2,300 visits usable because of missing images, loss to follow-up, poor segmentations. — [Kim RB et al. Comput Biol Med 2020;126:104042 [C]](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9058995) (accessed 2026-10-31). Patient-grouped folds; no external validation.
- **RE** An early wound-image CNN (EMBC 2015) claimed first automated long-term healing-progress prediction from wound images, using wound surface-area change over weeks as the predictive signal. — [A unified framework for automatic wound segmentation and analysis with deep CNNs, PubMed 26736781 [C]](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26736781/) (accessed 2026-10-31; abstract-level; dataset size not verified)

### Inferences
- **INF — Data-size reasoning NOW:** 14 patients; even if every patient had ≥2 dates, independent transitions ≤ 13–14 per type at the patient level (the patient, not the photo pair, is the unit; Li et al. deliberately used one comparison per patient in test sets). Splitting into worsening / stable / improving gives single-digit counts per class; any AUC would have a CI spanning chance. Also, hospital photos are likely taken at the point of suspected phlebitis, and catheter removal follows diagnosis, so "next photo" may be after removal — natural history is truncated by care (Cochrane/INS context: catheters are removed when phlebitis is identified). Therefore prediction of worsening is **not feasible NOW**; claiming it would risk fabricated performance.
- **INF — What is realistic NOW:**
  1. Build the longitudinal data model: patient pseudonym ID, limb/site code (LH/RH/LL/RL), insertion time, photo time (relative hours), catheter episode ID (a new catheter = new episode), stage and VIP/INS item scores per photo, "catheter removed at" time.
  2. Descriptive serial plots per patient: model outputs (probability of phlebitis, ordinal score) across dates, displayed in the UI as **research-only trend**, never as prognosis.
  3. Consistency check: does the frozen-feature model give monotone scores across a patient's dated photos when human stage is monotone? This is a sanity check, not a validated metric.
  4. Leakage: in all classification CV, photos from the same patient across dates must stay in the same fold (already done via StratifiedGroupKFold) — longitudinal photos are the main leakage hazard.
- **INF — FUTURE (~1,000 photos):** feasibility depends on the number of **patients with ≥2 dated photos on the same catheter episode**, not photo count. Rough targets by analogy to Li et al. (test sets of 65–272 patients; training on hundreds–thousands of patients): ≥100 such patients with ≥20–30 observed worsening transitions before even a frozen-feature pairwise model (difference of DINOv2 embeddings of a registered/cropped lesion pair → logistic regression for "worse vs not worse") is evaluable. Fine-tuning a Siamese CNN end-to-end would need several hundred patients. These thresholds are this report's judgement, not evidence-derived.
- **INF — Registration/alignment:** serial forearm photos differ in pose, distance, lighting and white balance; options: (a) use the confirmed lesion box/crop around the catheter site as the alignment anchor (like Li's joint cropping), (b) ArUco card in each photo for scale + colour normalisation, (c) feature-based homography (OpenCV ORB/SIFT) only for small pose changes. Without (b), colour-based change (Δa*) across dates is confounded by lighting.
- **INF — Label definition:** define change at the catheter-episode level: Δstage between consecutive photos (−1/0/+1/+2), plus time interval; require both photos rated by the same protocol; ideally a second rater for a subset to estimate how much apparent change is rater noise (kappa ~0.4–0.6 per Ray-Barruel).
- **INF — GPU:** frozen-embedding + logistic/pairwise models run on CPU; a Siamese ResNet-18/EfficientNet-B0 fine-tune at 224 px would fit 8 GB (unverified for this config).
- **INF — Principles check:** any "predicted to worsen" output is a model suggestion; must not trigger alerts or be described as clinical prediction. No conflict if it stays a research-only, human-reviewed display.
