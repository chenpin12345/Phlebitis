# Diffusion synthetic data, and few-shot / metric learning / similar-case retrieval, for a tiny phlebitis-photo research platform

Scope: (A) diffusion-generated synthetic training data; (B) few-shot / metric learning on frozen foundation features and similar-case retrieval. Each method is judged against the project: research prototype, not a diagnostic tool; about 120 whole-arm/hand photos from 14 patients (binary: positive 47 / normal 9; stage: normal 9 / stage 1 23 / stage 2 24); about 24 photos with lesion boxes; one annotator; patient-grouped 3-fold CV; all compute local on one consumer NVIDIA GPU.

Conventions used in these notes
- Source grades: [A] official docs, regulators or standards; [B] systematic reviews or top-venue peer review (Nature Medicine, CVPR, ICLR, ECCV, NeurIPS main track, CHI, USENIX Security); [C] other peer-reviewed; [D] preprints and workshop papers; [E] blogs, example code, model cards, README files (supporting only).
- Access date: every URL was accessed on 2026-10-31 unless noted otherwise. (The research started on 2026-10-30, was interrupted by rate limits, and was completed on 2026-10-31.)
- Evidence labels: "Research evidence shows" = stated in the cited source. "Industry practice" = official docs or tooling conventions. "This report's inference" = my reasoning. It is never presented as a finding.
- "Leakage / external validation" notes: added for each small-sample medical study, as far as the abstract or page allowed checking. "Not verifiable from abstract" means I could not see the full methods.

## (A1) Does diffusion-generated synthetic data improve classifiers in dermatology / skin, wounds and other small medical datasets? Effect sizes and the real-data size where the benefit appears or disappears

### Takeaway
Research evidence shows some benefit from diffusion synthetic data in dermatology. The gains are modest, uneven across diseases, and consistently smaller than the gains from adding real images. The largest published effects (Ktena et al., Nature Medicine 2024) came from generators trained on large labelled and unlabelled corpora, not on a few dozen images. No study I found shows a benefit when the generator is trained on about 50 images from about 14 patients. For phlebitis photos specifically, 未找到可靠證據.

### Cited Findings
- **[B] Ktena et al. 2024, Nature Medicine.**
  - Reference: Ktena I, Wiles O, Albuquerque I, Rebuffi S-A, Tanno R, Guha Roy A, Azizi S, Belgrave D, Kohli P, Karthikesalingam A, Cemgil T, Gowal S. "Generative models improve fairness of medical classifiers under distribution shifts." Nat Med 30:1166–1173 (2024). DOI 10.1038/s41591-024-02838-6. Preprint: arXiv 2304.09218.
  - Method: label-conditioned diffusion models learn "realistic augmentations" from abundant unlabelled data. The synthetic samples are added to real training data.
  - Effect sizes, out-of-distribution, from the abstract:
    - Histopathology: +7.7% accuracy.
    - Chest X-ray: +5.2% accuracy and a 44.6% smaller fairness gap.
    - Dermatology: +63.5% high-risk sensitivity and a 7.5× smaller fairness gap.
  - The authors say the approach does not replace representative real data.
  - Sources: [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09218); [Google Research page](https://research.google/pubs/generative-models-improve-fairness-of-medical-classifiers-under-distribution-shifts/).
  - Leakage / external validation: OOD test sets from other sites or countries serve as external validation. Patient-level split details are not verifiable from the abstract. The nature.com full text was paywall-redirected, and the arXiv PDF could not be text-extracted locally.
- **[D] Sagers et al. 2022, NeurIPS 2022 workshop on synthetic data** (workshop paper, so graded D).
  - Reference: Sagers LW, Diao JA, Groh M, Rajpurkar P, Adamson AS, Manrai AK. "Improving dermatology classifiers across populations using images generated by large diffusion models." arXiv 2211.13352.
  - Method: DALL·E 2 inpainting variations created from 8 seed images per condition, drawn from the lightest and darkest Fitzpatrick skin types, on the Fitzpatrick17k benchmark.
  - Result: overall classification improved, with the largest gains for underrepresented groups. The abstract gives no numbers.
  - Note: DALL·E 2 is a cloud service, so this method is not allowed for project photos.
  - Sources: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13352); [search summary confirming the 8-seed design](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2211.13352).
  - Leakage: Fitzpatrick17k comes from atlas websites with no patient IDs, so patient-level leakage cannot be ruled out. No external validation is stated.
- **[D] Sagers et al. 2023, arXiv 2308.12453.**
  - Reference: Sagers LW, Diao JA, Melas-Kyriazi L, Groh M, Rajpurkar P, Adamson AS, Rotemberg V, Daneshjou R, Manrai AK. "Augmenting medical image classifiers with synthetic data from latent diffusion models." DOI 10.48550/arXiv.2308.12453.
  - Scale: 458,920 synthetic images, made with several latent-diffusion generation strategies.
  - Results:
    - Synthetic images improve performance "in data-limited settings".
    - Gains plateau at synthetic-to-real ratios above 10:1.
    - Gains are "substantially smaller" than gains from adding real images.
  - Experimental design (from the search snippet of the PDF): real images per task varied from 1 to 228 for a 9-way skin task; up to 10 synthetic images per real one; accuracy averaged over 5 runs; 360-image held-out test set. Gains "varied by disease condition and generation method."
  - Sources: [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12453); [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.12453).
  - Leakage / external validation: the test set is held out from the same source. Patient-level grouping is not verifiable. No external validation is stated.
- **[D] Akrout et al. 2023, arXiv 2301.04802.** "Diffusion-based Data Augmentation for Skin Disease Classification: Impact Across Original Medical Datasets to Fully Synthetic Images" (12 authors).
  - Method: a text-to-image diffusion model with prompt control on a macroscopic (clinical-photo) skin dataset.
  - Result: a classifier trained on fully synthetic data keeps "similar" accuracy.
  - The abstract gives no numbers or dataset size.
  - Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.04802).
  - Leakage / external validation: not verifiable from the abstract.
- **[D] Derm-T2IM, DermaFlux and related work** (from search summaries only; the full texts were not verified).
  - DreamBooth plus LoRA fine-tunes of Stable Diffusion, tested on ISIC and HAM10000. A hybrid of synthetic and real data beat real data alone.
  - DermaFlux (LoRA on Flux.1) reports "up to 6%" gains when augmenting small real datasets.
  - Source: [search summary page, arXiv 2603.16392](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16392).
  - Treat these as unverified preprint claims.
- **[B] Wang J, Zhang Y, Ding Z, Hamm J. "Doctor Approved: Generating Medically Accurate Skin Disease Images through AI-Expert Feedback" (MAGIC), NeurIPS 2025** (per the arXiv comments field). arXiv 2506.12323.
  - The authors state that diffusion models "frequently produce medically inaccurate images", which degrades diagnostic models.
  - Their fix uses multimodal LLM evaluators guided by expert criteria.
  - Reported gains: +9.02% accuracy on a 20-condition task, and +13.89% in the few-shot setting.
  - Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12323).
  - Leakage / external validation: not verifiable from the abstract.
  - Possible conflict with project rules: if the MLLM evaluator is a cloud API, using it on project images would breach the no-cloud rule. Whether the paper used cloud or local MLLMs was not verified.
- **Wound images and other small medical datasets:** I found no verified, peer-reviewed study in this research round that quantifies diffusion augmentation on wound or IV-site photos. 未找到可靠證據 (wound-specific).
- **Phlebitis imaging context:**
  - The only photo-based AI phlebitis study found is a trial registration, NCT07889895. It plans mobile-phone photos of PIVC sites graded on the Visual Infusion Phlebitis (VIP) score, with a held-out test set. No results are posted. Source: [ClinicalTrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07889895).
  - Other PIVC phlebitis machine-learning work uses tabular clinical variables or ultrasound, not photographs. Source: [PMC11295139](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11295139/).

### Inferences
- **This report's inference:** every positive dermatology result above used one of three things: large real training sets (Fitzpatrick17k, ISIC or HAM10000, thousands of images), large pretrained text-to-image models, or massive unlabelled pools (Ktena). The phlebitis project has none of these conditions. Extrapolating the published effect sizes (5–63% relative gains) to 14 patients is not justified.
- **This report's inference:** Sagers 2023 is the most relevant dose-response evidence, since it varied real images from 1 to 228 per task. Even there, synthetic images were a partial substitute, and adding real images beat adding synthetic ones. For the project, each additional real patient is likely worth more than any amount of synthetic data.
- **This report's inference:** phlebitis staging depends on subtle cues: redness extent, swelling, a palpable cord, and the relation to the cannula. Generic text-to-image models (Stable Diffusion) have no learned concept of "stage 2 phlebitis". A fine-tuned model would learn it only from about 24 stage-2 photos of a handful of patients, so label fidelity cannot be assumed (see A2).

### Gaps
- I could not extract dermatology-specific dataset sizes, the generator design, or the curve of benefit against the number of labelled images from Ktena et al. The nature.com full text was paywall-redirected, and the arXiv PDF was binary that local tools could not parse.
- I could not verify numeric results for Akrout et al., Derm-T2IM or DermaFlux beyond their abstracts or snippets.
- I found no study of diffusion augmentation for wound, IV-site or phlebitis photographs (未找到可靠證據).
- I found no study that reports a minimum real-data size at which diffusion augmentation starts to help in dermatology. Sagers 2023 is the closest, but its abstract gives no threshold.

## (A2) Failure / negative evidence: memorization, artifacts, label fidelity, evaluation contamination, and fine-tuning on about 50 images / 14 patients

### Takeaway
Research evidence shows that diffusion models memorize and replicate training images. The effect is stronger with small training sets, text conditioning and over-training. Medical-specific studies report a large fraction of patients memorized. Standard metrics such as FID and Inception Score do not detect memorization. Fine-tuning a diffusion model on about 50 images of 14 patients therefore carries a high risk of producing near-copies of identifiable patients' arms and hands. Such copies add little new information and can leak into evaluation.

### Cited Findings
- **[B] Carlini N, et al. "Extracting Training Data from Diffusion Models." USENIX Security 2023, pp. 5253–5270.** arXiv 2301.13188.
  - A generate-and-filter attack extracted "over a thousand" training examples from state-of-the-art diffusion models, including personal photos.
  - Sources: [USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity23/presentation/carlini); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13188).
  - A secondary summary gives "109 images from Stable Diffusion". This number was not verified, so do not cite it.
- **[B] Somepalli G, et al. "Diffusion Art or Digital Forgery? Investigating Data Replication in Diffusion Models." CVPR 2023.** arXiv 2212.03860.
  - Experiments on Oxford Flowers, CelebA, ImageNet and LAION show that replication depends strongly on training-set size: small datasets lead to frequent, blatant copying.
  - Text-conditioned models replicate far more than unconditional ones.
  - Stable Diffusion recombines memorized foreground and background objects.
  - Mitigation: randomize or augment captions during training and inference.
  - Sources: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03860); [CVPR poster page](https://cvpr.thecvf.com/virtual/2023/poster/21082).
- **[D] Dar SUH, et al. "Unconditional Latent Diffusion Models Memorize Patient Imaging Data: Implications for Openly Sharing Synthetic Data."** arXiv 2402.01054 (v1 Feb 2024; v3 Jan 2025). 14 authors including Truhn, Kather and Engelhardt.
  - Setup: 2D and 3D latent diffusion models trained on CT, MR and X-ray data. Memorization was measured with a self-supervised copy-detection model.
  - Results vary by version:
    - v1: up to 41.7% (CT), 19.6% (MR) and 32.6% (X-ray) of training data memorized.
    - v2: about 40.9% of patient data memorized, and 78.5% of synthetic samples identified as patient copies.
    - Text attributed to the published version by a search summary: 37.2% of patient data and 68.7% of synthetic samples. The venue of that version was not verified, so cite the arXiv version you quote.
  - Augmentation, smaller architectures and more training data reduce memorization. Over-training increases it.
  - Diffusion models memorize more than GANs or autoencoders but synthesize better.
  - Sources: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01054); [TU Dresden record](https://fis.tu-dresden.de/portal/en/publications/unconditional-latent-diffusion-models-memorize-patient-imaging-data(f0f67a44-3658-42c4-aff2-36fe07eac25e).html).
- **[D] Dar SUH, Ghanaat A, et al. "Investigating Data Memorization in 3D Latent Diffusion Models for Medical Image Synthesis."** arXiv 2307.01148 (2023).
  - 3D LDMs trained on photon-counting coronary CTA and the MRNet knee MRI dataset "indeed memorize training data."
  - Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01148v2).
- **[C] Akbar MU, Wang W, Eklund A. "Beware of diffusion models for synthesizing medical images — A comparison with GANs in terms of memorizing brain MRI and chest x-ray images."** Machine Learning: Science and Technology, 2025 (venue per search summary). arXiv 2305.07644.
  - Diffusion models memorized training images more than StyleGAN, especially for small datasets.
  - FID and Inception Score "are not suitable" for detecting memorization.
  - Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07644).
- **[B] Wang et al. 2025 (MAGIC, NeurIPS 2025)** state that diffusion models "frequently produce medically inaccurate images" of skin disease, which degrades classifier performance. This is direct label-fidelity evidence. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12323).
- **[D] Sagers et al. 2023:** gains varied by disease and generation method, which means some conditions or methods gave little or no benefit. Gains plateau above a 10:1 synthetic-to-real ratio. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12453).
- **[A/E] Official diffusers DreamBooth documentation:** "DreamBooth is very sensitive to training hyperparameters, and it is easy to overfit." Source: [Hugging Face diffusers docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/training/dreambooth).

### Inferences
- **This report's inference (memorization at the project's scale):** the training fold of each CV split holds about 9–10 patients and about 80 photos. A text-conditioned DreamBooth or LoRA fine-tune on that set is the worst case in the Somepalli and Dar findings: small data, text conditioning, many steps per image. Expect near-copies of specific patients' hands, tattoos, IV dressings and backgrounds. Training a classifier on near-copies is close to duplicating real images, so the expected gain is small. It may also amplify patient-specific shortcuts such as background, lighting, the hospital's dressing type or the LINE photo style.
- **This report's inference (label fidelity):**
  - A generator conditioned on "stage 2" learns whatever correlates with stage 2 in about 24 images. In this project, stage labels for hospital photos come from filename rules, and LINE photos were reviewed by a single annotator. So the generator also learns label noise and source cues. For example, if most stage-2 photos come from one source, the "stage 2" concept may encode the source style rather than the pathology.
  - Without at least one independent clinical reviewer, nobody can confirm that a synthetic "stage 2" image shows stage-2 phlebitis. A wrongly generated stage would amount to writing an unverified label into the training data. That conflicts with the project rule against fabricating labels unless every synthetic image is human-confirmed.
- **This report's inference (evaluation contamination):** if the generator sees any validation or test patient, even only through a generator trained once on all 120 photos, synthetic training images can carry that patient's appearance into training. Cross-validation scores would then be inflated. This is the same leakage as a patient-level split violation, only harder to see.
- **This report's inference (privacy):** the project does not plan to share synthetic images. However, memorized near-copies of identifiable hands or arms (tattoos, jewellery, skin marks) are still research photos for governance purposes. They should be stored and handled under the same rules as real photos.

### Gaps
- I found no study of diffusion fine-tuning on fewer than about 100 medical images with patient-grouped evaluation (未找到可靠證據). The closest are Sagers 2023 (as few as 1 real image per class, but with a large pretrained generator and an image-level test) and the DreamBooth subject-fidelity literature, which is not medical.
- I found no quantitative label-fidelity study (blinded expert staging of synthetic images) for any vascular or inflammatory skin condition (未找到可靠證據).
- I did not verify which journal published the final Dar et al. version.

## (A3) Safe-use protocol for synthetic data, and cheaper alternatives (classical, color/illumination, copy-paste, mixup/CutMix)

### Takeaway
If synthetic images are ever used, they must be training-only and generated per fold from that fold's training patients. They must never enter validation or test, must be provenance-tagged, must be checked for memorization with embedding nearest-neighbor distance, and must be confirmed by a human before entering training. FID or precision-recall cannot be trusted at n≈100. Classical, color and illumination augmentation, plus mixup or CutMix, are cheaper and better supported for small medical sets and should come first.

### Cited Findings
- **[C/D] Perez F, Vasconcelos C, Avila S, Valle E. "Data Augmentation for Skin Lesion Analysis." ISIC Skin Image Analysis Workshop 2018** (workshop, so graded D/C). arXiv 1809.01442.
  - 13 augmentation scenarios (color, geometric, elastic, random erasing, lesion mixing) on 3 CNNs. The best scenario reached AUC 0.882 without external data, beating the ISIC 2017 winner at 0.874, which used extra data.
  - Augmentation helped both in training and at test time.
  - The authors claim augmentation "can yield more performance gains than acquiring new images." This is the authors' claim, for dermoscopy, so do not generalize it.
  - Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.01442).
  - Leakage: the study uses ISIC 2017 official splits. Lesion and patient duplication across splits was not assessed in the abstract. The images are dermoscopic, not whole-arm photos.
- **[D] Rao A, et al. "Studying the Impact of Augmentations on Medical Confidence Calibration." ICCV 2023 Workshop (CVAMD).**
  - MixUp, CutMix and CutOut were tested on skin, CT, chest X-ray and MRI data.
  - CutMix improved calibration the most. CutOut often worsened calibration.
  - Accuracy and AUROC generally improved, with exceptions such as ResNet-101 on MRI tumor and ResNet-50 on CT COVID.
  - Sources: [CVF open access](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023W/CVAMD/html/Rao_Studying_the_Impact_of_Augmentations_on_Medical_Confidence_Calibration_ICCVW_2023_paper.html); [arXiv 2308.11902](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.11902).
  - Dataset sizes were not verified.
- **Contrasting natural-image evidence:**
  - [B] A NeurIPS 2019 paper (attribution to Thulasidasan et al. is unverified; see the reference list): mixup improves calibration, largely through label smoothing. Source: [NeurIPS PDF](https://papers.NeurIPS.cc/paper_files/paper/2019/file/36ad8b5f42db492827016448975cc22d-Paper.pdf).
  - [D] The AutoMix preprint reports that the Cut family can aggravate overconfidence, while MixUp can cause under-confidence. Source: [arXiv 2103.13027](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.13027).
  - So the direction of calibration effects depends on the setting. Re-measure it on project data.
- **[D] Pizzi E, Dutta Roy S, Ravindra SN, Goyal P, Douze M. "A Self-Supervised Descriptor for Image Copy Detection" (SSCD).** arXiv 2202.10261 (2022).
  - A copy-detection descriptor that beats SimCLR descriptors by 48% absolute on DISC2021. It is a purpose-built tool for near-duplicate detection.
  - Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10261).
  - It appeared at CVPR 2022 per common citation, but the arXiv page does not state a venue, so label it a preprint.
- **[D] Dar et al. 2024/2025** used a self-supervised copy-detection model to flag memorized samples. This supports nearest-neighbor checks in self-supervised embedding space as current research practice. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01054).
- **[C] Akbar et al. 2025:** FID and Inception Score are unsuitable for detecting memorization. Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07644).
- **[A/E] Hugging Face diffusers DreamBooth guide.** Source: [docs](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/training/dreambooth).
  - Industry practice:
    - Prior-preservation loss retains the model's class knowledge.
    - Example settings: lr 5e-6, 400 steps, 512 px, batch size 1.
    - LoRA weights are about 100 MB.
  - VRAM, from the docs:
    - 16 GB: 8-bit Adam plus gradient checkpointing.
    - 12 GB: additionally xFormers memory-efficient attention and `set_grads_to_none`.
    - 8 GB: needs DeepSpeed stage 2 with CPU offload and about 25 GB of system RAM.
    - Training the text encoder needs ≥24 GB.
  - Note: the doc's example uses `--push_to_hub`. That flag must not be used here because it uploads to the cloud.
- **Copy-paste of lesion crops:** I did not verify a medical-image copy-paste study in this research round. The general instance-segmentation evidence (Ghiasi et al., CVPR 2021) was not fetched. 未找到可靠證據 (verified, medical, small-data).

### Inferences — proposed safe-use protocol (this report's inference, built from the cited risks)
1. **Per-fold generation.** For each StratifiedGroupKFold split, and for the held-out test split, train or fine-tune the generator only on that split's training patients. Generate synthetic images only for that fold's training set. Never train one generator on all 120 photos.
2. **Training-only.** Synthetic images never enter validation, test, threshold selection, calibration, Grad-CAM galleries used as evidence, or reported metrics. Report every metric on real images only.
3. **Provenance in SQLite.** Store `is_synthetic`, `generator_id`, `generator_train_patient_ids`, `fold_id`, `seed`, `prompt`/`condition`, `parent_real_image_id` (for inpainting or ControlNet), `nn_distance_dinov2`, `nn_distance_sscd`, `review_status`, `reviewer` and `review_date`.
   - Enforce in code that any image with `is_synthetic=1` is excluded from evaluation queries.
   - The UI must show a clear "合成影像" badge.
4. **Human review before use.** A reviewer, ideally a clinician rather than the single annotator, confirms that each synthetic image is plausible and matches its conditioned stage. Rejected images are kept with a reason, for audit. This follows the project rule that model outputs must be human-confirmed before being written.
5. **Memorization check.** For each synthetic image, compute cosine distance to every real training image in DINOv2 and SSCD embedding space (and, ideally, in pixel or LPIPS space). Flag and drop images whose nearest-neighbor similarity exceeds a threshold calibrated on real-vs-real pairs from the same patient. Same-patient photos taken on different days form a natural "near-duplicate" reference distribution.
6. **Ablation as the only accepted evidence.** Compare real-only training against real-plus-synthetic training under the identical patient-grouped CV, with the same seeds. Report the paired difference with bootstrap CIs. With 14 patients, CIs will likely span zero, and that must be reported as "no demonstrated benefit."
7. **Do not trust FID or precision-recall at n≈100.** They are unstable with small reference sets and blind to memorization (Akbar et al.). At most, use them descriptively.
8. **Cloud conflict.** DALL·E 2 inpainting (Sagers 2022) and any hosted MLLM judge (a possible MAGIC-style setup) would send research photos to third parties, which breaches project rules. The local alternatives are diffusers Stable Diffusion, SDXL or Flux LoRA running on the local GPU, and human review instead of an MLLM judge.

- **This report's inference (cheaper alternatives, recommended first):**
  - Geometric augmentation: flip (note that a flip swaps the LH/RH appearance; the label is unaffected), small rotations, random resized crop that keeps the IV site in frame.
  - Color and illumination augmentation: brightness, contrast, gamma, white-balance or temperature shifts, and mild hue jitter. Hue jitter must stay small, because the label depends on redness, so a strong hue shift could change the apparent stage. This suits the mix of hospital and LINE phone photos.
  - JPEG and blur augmentation to mimic LINE compression.
  - Mixup or CutMix: cheap. Calibration effects vary by setting (Rao 2023 vs AutoMix), so measure them.
  - Lesion copy-paste using the about 24 boxed lesions: possible but risky. Pasting a red patch onto a "normal" arm creates an unverified positive image, so it needs the same human-review and training-only rules as synthetic images.

### Gaps
- I found no verified study of copy-paste augmentation on small clinical-photo skin datasets (未找到可靠證據).
- I found no published memorization threshold (DINOv2 or SSCD cosine) validated for medical photos. The threshold must be set empirically from same-patient real pairs (this report's inference).
- The dataset sizes in Rao 2023 were not verified.
- I found no source for DreamBooth or LoRA training time on a consumer GPU at about 80 images. The diffusers LoRA guide was not fetched. 未找到可靠證據 for timing.

## (A4) Recommendation: NOW (~120 photos, 14 patients) vs FUTURE (~1,000 photos, three balanced classes)

### Takeaway
NOW: do not use diffusion synthetic data for training or evaluation. At most, run a sandboxed, clearly labelled feasibility experiment that measures memorization, and invest in classical and color augmentation instead. FUTURE: a per-fold, LoRA-fine-tuned latent diffusion or inpainting pipeline becomes a defensible ablation, with a synthetic-to-real ratio well below 10:1, human-reviewed images and strict provenance. Expect a small benefit at best.

### Cited Findings
- Gains are substantially smaller than adding real images and plateau above a 10:1 synthetic-to-real ratio — [Sagers 2023 [D]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12453).
- Small training sets lead to blatant replication — [Somepalli CVPR 2023 [B]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.03860).
- Medical LDMs memorize a large fraction of patients. More data and augmentation reduce this; over-training increases it — [Dar et al. [D]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01054).
- Generated skin images are often medically inaccurate — [Wang et al. NeurIPS 2025 [B]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.12323).
- Large benefits appear when generators exploit large unlabelled corpora and are tested OOD — [Ktena 2024 [B]](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.09218).
- VRAM: DreamBooth on SD 1.5 is feasible on 12–16 GB with memory optimizations; 8 GB needs DeepSpeed CPU offload — [diffusers docs [A/E]](https://huggingface.co/docs/diffusers/training/dreambooth).

### Inferences
**NOW (~120 photos / 14 patients) — this report's inference**
- Expected benefit: about zero, or negative. The generator would see about 9–10 patients per fold, roughly 6 normal photos per fold, and very few distinct patients per stage. Memorization risk is at the high end of the published evidence.
- Minimum data: no source gives a threshold, so this is inference only. Diffusion fine-tuning is unlikely to give non-memorized, label-faithful samples with fewer than tens of patients per class.
- Applicable: only as a methods-learning sandbox. For example, train a LoRA on one fold, generate images, measure DINOv2 and SSCD nearest-neighbor similarity, and show memorization to the team as a negative-result chapter. Synthetic images must not enter any reported model.
- GPU: SD 1.5 LoRA or DreamBooth fits 12–16 GB with 8-bit Adam and gradient checkpointing (docs). On 8 GB, use DeepSpeed offload with about 25 GB of system RAM (docs). SDXL or Flux generally needs more memory; I did not verify numbers (gap).
- Risks: memorized patient near-copies, a shortcut toward source style (hospital vs LINE), leakage through a generator trained on all images, and unverified stage labels. Any of these would conflict with the rules against fabricating labels and inflating performance.
- What to do instead now:
  1. Color, illumination and JPEG augmentation in the existing EfficientNet-B0 and DINOv2-logistic pipelines, with a patient-grouped ablation.
  2. Collect more patients, especially normal ones, which are the scarcest class at 9 photos.
  3. Consider lesion-centred crops for the ~24 boxed photos.
  4. Use kNN or prototype methods (see B).

**FUTURE (~1,000 photos, three balanced classes) — this report's inference**
- Applicable as a controlled ablation, with the per-fold protocol from A3.
- Prefer inpainting or ControlNet-style edits of real training images over free text-to-image generation. Inpainting changes only the IV-site region, so background and skin identity stay real and label shifts are bounded.
- Keep synthetic-to-real ratios at or below about 1:1–2:1. Sagers saw a plateau at 10:1 even on larger data.
- Expected benefit: small, possibly a few points of balanced accuracy on rare subgroups (for example, darker or lighter skin, LINE photos). This is not demonstrated for phlebitis and must be measured.
- Minimum data: 未找到可靠證據 for a phlebitis-specific threshold.
- Wiring:
  - An offline script (`tools/synth/`) that reads the fold manifest, trains a LoRA with `diffusers` `train_dreambooth_lora.py` locally (no `--push_to_hub`), generates images into a quarantined folder, and computes nearest-neighbor distances.
  - A FastAPI review queue (reuse the existing human-confirmation UI) where approved images get `review_status=approved`.
  - Training jobs opt in to synthetic images with an explicit flag. They are never on by default, which keeps the "no automatic training" rule.
- Risks: as for NOW, but lower memorization risk with more patients and augmentation (Dar et al.). Label fidelity still needs clinician review.

### Gaps
- No phlebitis-, wound- or IV-site-specific synthetic-data evidence was found (未找到可靠證據).
- VRAM and timing for SDXL or Flux LoRA on 8–16 GB were not verified.

## (B1) Few-shot and metric learning: prototypical networks, kNN on frozen foundation features, Siamese/triplet networks, SimpleShot, "A Closer Look", cross-domain failure of meta-learning, and medical/dermatology foundation models

### Takeaway
Research evidence consistently shows that simple methods match or beat episodic meta-learning, especially under domain shift that includes dermatology (BSCD-FSL). These simple methods are nearest-neighbor or prototype classifiers on good pretrained features, with centering and L2 normalization, or plain fine-tuning or linear probes. For the project, the evidence-aligned choice is frozen DINOv2 (or, after evaluation, a dermatology foundation model) with cosine kNN, nearest-class-mean prototypes or logistic regression. Siamese, triplet or meta-learning training on 14 patients is not advisable.

### Cited Findings
- **[B] Snell J, Swersky K, Zemel RS. "Prototypical Networks for Few-shot Learning."** arXiv 1703.05175.
  - Classifies by distance to class-mean prototypes in a learned metric space. The authors argue that this simpler inductive bias suits limited data, and that simple design choices beat more complex meta-learning approaches.
  - Venue: NeurIPS 2017 per proceedings. The arXiv page itself lists no venue, so cite the arXiv ID.
  - Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.05175).
- **[D] Wang Y, Chao W-L, Weinberger KQ, van der Maaten L. "SimpleShot: Revisiting Nearest-Neighbor Classification for Few-Shot Learning."** arXiv 1911.04623 (2019).
  - Mean-subtraction (centering) plus L2 normalization plus a nearest-neighbor or nearest-centroid classifier outperforms prior results in 3 of 5 miniImageNet settings, without meta-learning.
  - Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04623).
- **[B] Chen W-Y, Liu Y-C, Kira Z, Wang Y-CF, Huang J-B. "A Closer Look at Few-shot Classification." ICLR 2019.** arXiv 1904.04232.
  - A modified baseline is competitive with the state of the art. Deeper backbones shrink the gaps between methods.
  - In the cross-domain setting, standard fine-tuning "compares favorably" to few-shot methods.
  - Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.04232).
- **[B] Guo Y, Codella NC, Karlinsky L, Codella JV, Smith JR, Saenko K, Rosing T, Feris R. "A Broader Study of Cross-Domain Few-Shot Learning" (BSCD-FSL). ECCV 2020.** arXiv 1912.07200.
  - The benchmark covers crop-disease, satellite, dermatology and radiology images.
  - "All meta-learning methods underperform in relation to simple fine-tuning by 12.8% average accuracy."
  - Cross-domain-specific methods lose their gains.
  - Accuracy correlates with how similar a dataset is to natural images, so dermatology and radiology are the hardest.
  - Source: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.07200).
  - Leakage: episodes are sampled at image level from ISIC; patient-level grouping is not addressed (inference from benchmark design; not verified in the text).
- **[B-level venue, but README-reported numbers, so treat as E] Oquab et al. DINOv2, official repository.** Backbones and ImageNet results:

  | Backbone (distilled) | Params | ImageNet k-NN | ImageNet linear |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | ViT-S/14 | 21M | 79.0% | 81.1% |
  | ViT-B/14 | 86M | 82.1% | 84.5% |
  | ViT-L/14 | 300M | 83.5% | 86.3% |
  | ViT-g/14 (not distilled) | 1.1B | 83.5% | 86.5% |

  - Variants with registers exist (for example, ViT-S/14 with registers: 79.1% / 80.9%).
  - Code and weights are under Apache-2.0.
  - So kNN on frozen features comes within 2–3 points of a linear probe on natural images.
  - Source: [DINOv2 GitHub](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dinov2).
- **[D] Baharoon M, et al. "Evaluating General Purpose Vision Foundation Models for Medical Image Analysis: An Experimental Study of DINOv2 on Radiology Benchmarks."** arXiv 2312.02366 (v4 Sep 2024).
  - Covers 9 public X-ray, CT and MRI benchmarks, evaluated with kNN, few-shot, linear probe, full fine-tuning and parameter-efficient fine-tuning.
  - DINOv2 is "competitive" for disease classification and superior for segmentation.
  - Specific kNN numbers were not retrieved.
  - Sources: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.02366); code at github.com/MohammedSB/DINOv2ForRadiology (cited in the abstract; not fetched).
- **[B] Yan S, et al. PanDerm, "A multimodal vision foundation model for clinical dermatology." Nature Medicine 2025.** DOI 10.1038/s41591-025-03747-y.
  - Pretraining: about 2.15M unlabelled images from 11 institutions and 4 modalities (35.3% total-body-photography tiles, 25.4% dermatopathology, 21.4% clinical, 17.9% dermoscopic).
  - Performance:
    - Often beats existing models using only 5–10% of labels. For some tasks, matching other models needed 10–30% of labels.
    - Linear probing was "comparable" to full fine-tuning (Supplementary Table 2).
    - Beat a DINOv2 ViT-L baseline.
  - Clinical-photo tasks include DermNet (23 classes), MMT-09 and MMT-74.
  - Stated limitations: about 200 conditions covered; mostly overall accuracy rather than per-disease analysis; many private datasets.
  - Sources: [PMC12353815](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12353815/).
  - A search summary claimed 83.1% linear probe vs 85.4% fine-tune on HAM10000. This could not be confirmed in the fetched text, so do not cite it.
- **[E] PanDerm GitHub README.**
  - Weights: PanDerm ViT-L/16 and PanDerm_Base ViT-B/16 on Google Drive; DermLIP_PanDerm on Hugging Face.
  - Checkpoints are `.pth` files.
  - License: CC-BY-NC-ND 4.0, non-commercial academic research only.
  - Linear-probe scripts (`linear_eval.py`) are provided; kNN scripts are not mentioned.
  - Source: [GitHub](https://github.com/SiyuanYan1/PanDerm).
- **[E] Google Derm Foundation model card.**
  - Architecture: BiT-M ResNet101x3. Output: 6,144-dimensional embeddings. Input: 448×448 PNG.
  - Training images include smartphone and teledermatology clinical photos taken by clinicians or patients.
  - Data efficiency: linear classifiers on its embeddings beat a standard BiT-M by a reported 10–15% accuracy across training sizes.
  - Access: gated on Hugging Face under the Health AI Developer Foundations (HAI-DEF) terms. Local inference is supported.
  - Source: [Hugging Face model card](https://huggingface.co/google/derm-foundation).
- **MONET (dermatology CLIP):** mentioned in PanDerm only as a teacher candidate. A Derm1M README reports DermLIP-PanDerm at 69.6% vs MONET at 66.9% on a 10%-label linear probe. Both numbers come from the authors' own repository, so they are [E]. Source: [Derm1M GitHub](https://github.com/SiyuanYan1/Derm1M).
- **Siamese and triplet networks for small medical photo sets:** I verified no study in this round. 未找到可靠證據 (verified, small-sample, patient-grouped).

### Inferences
- **This report's inference:** BSCD-FSL, SimpleShot and Chen 2019 together say that the representation matters far more than the few-shot algorithm. The project already has DINOv2-small with logistic regression. Adding SimpleShot-style centering (subtract the training-fold mean), L2 normalization, a cosine kNN (k in {1, 3, 5, 7}) and a nearest-class-mean prototype classifier costs almost nothing. It gives an interpretable baseline whose prediction can be explained by the nearest neighbors (see B2).
- **This report's inference:** training a Siamese, triplet or episodic meta-learner on 14 patients would mostly learn patient identity, because same-patient photos are the most similar pairs. Pairs and triplets would need cross-patient positives only. Even then, with about 9–10 training patients per fold, the learned metric is unlikely to beat frozen DINOv2 and is expensive to validate.
- **This report's inference (dermatology foundation models):**
  - PanDerm and Derm Foundation are trained on skin-disease images, including clinical photos. They may separate erythema and swelling better than DINOv2, which was trained on natural images. Whether that holds for arm or IV-site phlebitis is unknown, and per project rules they must be fully evaluated on project data before use.
  - Possible conflicts with the safetensors-only import rule and with licenses:
    - PanDerm ships `.pth` files, which would need offline conversion to safetensors after inspection.
    - Its CC-BY-NC-ND license may restrict distributing converted or fine-tuned derivatives. That is a legal question, flagged here, not resolved.
    - Derm Foundation requires accepting HAI-DEF terms on Hugging Face. Accepting terms is the user's own action.
    - The weight format and framework for Derm Foundation were not verified; it may be a TensorFlow model.
- **This report's inference (whole image vs crops):** the classifier input is the whole photo, and the lesion occupies a small area. A global CLS embedding may be dominated by background, the dressing, or the hand pose. A crop-based embedding of the YOLO box or a manually confirmed box, or the patch-token mean inside the box, would likely be more lesion-specific. It should be compared head-to-head under the same patient-grouped CV.

### Gaps
- No verified kNN or few-shot numbers for DINOv2 on dermatology clinical photos (the radiology numbers in Baharoon were not retrieved).
- No published evaluation of foundation-model kNN for phlebitis, wounds or IV sites (未找到可靠證據).
- No head-to-head Siamese or triplet vs kNN study on small, patient-grouped skin photo sets (未找到可靠證據).
- Derm Foundation's weight format and framework were not verified.

## (B2) Similar-case retrieval (content-based image retrieval) as decision support: evidence, risks and safeguards

### Takeaway
Research evidence shows that AI decision support can improve clinician accuracy, most for less experienced users. In the one large dermatology reader study, however, retrieved similar images were less useful than AI class probabilities, and raters tended to ignore retrieval over time. In pathology, retrieval with user-refinement tools increased trust and diagnostic utility without lowering accuracy. Risks include automation bias (faulty AI misled even experts), same-patient retrieval inflating apparent accuracy, and privacy of showing other people's photos.

### Cited Findings
- **[B] Tschandl P, Rinner C, Apalla Z, Argenziano G, Codella N, … Kittler H. "Human–computer collaboration for skin cancer recognition." Nature Medicine 26(8):1229–1234 (2020).** DOI 10.1038/s41591-020-0942-0; PMID 32572267.
  - Good-quality AI support improves accuracy over either AI or physicians alone, with the least experienced clinicians gaining most.
  - AI multiclass probabilities outperformed content-based image retrieval in the mobile setting.
  - Faulty AI can mislead clinicians across the experience spectrum, including experts.
  - Sources: [Europe PMC record](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/32572267); [MedUni Wien press release](https://www.meduniwien.ac.at/web/en/about-us/news/detailsite/2020/news-im-juni-2020/study-illustrates-huge-potential-of-humanartificial-intelligence-collaboration-in-medicine/).
  - A search snippet reports that raters "tended to ignore the AI-based CBIR decision support over time." That snippet came from a repository copy of the paper; the exact wording was not verified in the full text.
  - Numbers not verified: exact accuracy gains, the number of raters, and any "13% increase" figure from a press release (internally contradictory). Do not cite numbers for this study beyond the abstract.
  - Leakage / external validation: this is a reader study on held-out images, not a model-validation study.
- **[B] Cai CJ, Reif E, Hegde N, Hipp J, Kim B, Smilkov D, Wattenberg M, Viégas F, Corrado GS, Stumpe MC, Terry M. "Human-Centered Tools for Coping with Imperfect Algorithms during Medical Decision-Making." CHI 2019.** arXiv 1902.02960.
  - SMILY is a DNN-based similar-image search tool for pathology.
  - No algorithm captures an expert's notion of similarity perfectly, so the authors added end-user refinement tools (for example, refine by region or by example).
  - In two pathologist studies, refinement increased the diagnostic utility of retrieved images and user trust, with no loss in diagnostic accuracy. Experts preferred it to a traditional interface.
  - Users repurposed the tools to probe the algorithm and to tell ML errors from their own errors.
  - Sources: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.02960); [Google Research page](https://research.google/pubs/human-centered-tools-for-coping-with-imperfect-algorithms-during-medical-decision-making/).
  - The full author list above is my recollection and was not fully verified. Cite as "Cai CJ, et al."
- **[B] PanDerm (Nature Medicine 2025) reader study 3** (128 conditions, clinical photos): AI assistance raised top-3 accuracy from 54% to 63.4%. This is classification assistance, not retrieval. Source: [PMC12353815](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12353815/).

### Inferences
- **This report's inference (fit to the project):**
  - Similar-case retrieval suits a research prototype better than a stage label, because it shows evidence instead of asserting a diagnosis. The UI should present it as reference cases ("相似案例參考"), not as a prediction.
  - Tschandl's finding that probabilities beat retrieval means retrieval should not be presented as superior decision support. With only 14 patients, the neighbors are few and often unrepresentative.
- **This report's inference — risks and mitigations:**
  1. **Same-patient retrieval.** The nearest neighbors of a photo will almost always be the same patient's other photos (other dates, other hand). This inflates kNN accuracy and makes the display uninformative. Mitigations:
     - Always exclude the query's own `patient_id` at query time and in evaluation.
     - In CV, the index contains only training-fold patients.
  2. **Leakage.** The index must be rebuilt per fold. Any centering mean or normalization statistics (SimpleShot) are computed on the training fold only.
  3. **Automation bias.** Faulty AI misled experts (Tschandl). Mitigations:
     - Show neighbors with their human-confirmed labels and similarity scores.
     - Show cases from all classes, for example the top-k per class, rather than only the top-k overall, to counter confirmation.
     - Add a disclaimer that this is a research prototype and not a diagnosis.
     - Log whether the user's final label differs from the neighbors' majority.
  4. **Privacy.** Showing other patients' photos (hospital and LINE users) to a viewer is a secondary use of personal health images. Mitigations:
     - Restrict display to authorized research staff.
     - Show crops of the IV site where possible.
     - Blur faces, tattoos and identifying marks.
     - Never show LINE users' photos to other LINE users.
     - Check consent and IRB scope for display.
  5. **Similarity ≠ clinical similarity** (SMILY). Offer refinement, such as crop-based vs whole-image search, and same-source vs any-source filtering.

### Gaps
- Exact effect sizes from Tschandl 2020 (accuracy with vs without AI, and the CBIR condition) could not be verified; nature.com was paywall-redirected.
- No study of CBIR for phlebitis, wounds or IV sites was found (未找到可靠證據).
- No dermatology CBIR study with patient-level exclusion explicitly reported was verified in this round.

## (B3) Implementation for this stack, GPU estimate, and NOW vs FUTURE recommendation

### Takeaway
NOW: add a frozen-DINOv2 cosine-kNN and prototype classifier plus a same-patient-excluded similar-case viewer, evaluated with patient-grouped CV. It is cheap, runs on CPU or any GPU, and is well supported by the few-shot literature. FUTURE: compare DINOv2-small, DINOv2-base and a dermatology foundation model (after license and format checks), crop vs whole-image embeddings, and kNN vs logistic regression vs light fine-tuning. Expect linear or fine-tuned models to overtake kNN as data grows.

### Cited Findings
- DINOv2 ViT-S/14 has 21M parameters and ViT-B/14 has 86M. ImageNet kNN is within about 2–3 points of the linear probe. Apache-2.0 — [DINOv2 GitHub [E]](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dinov2).
- FAISS is MIT-licensed, has faiss-cpu and faiss-gpu packages via Anaconda, and supports cosine similarity as inner product on normalized vectors. The README does not mention Windows; check INSTALL.md — [FAISS GitHub [E]](https://github.com/facebookresearch/faiss).
- Centering plus L2 normalization plus nearest neighbor is a strong few-shot baseline — [SimpleShot [D]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.04623).
- Simple fine-tuning beats meta-learning by 12.8% under cross-domain shift — [BSCD-FSL [B]](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.07200).
- Dermatology foundation-model linear probes are comparable to fine-tuning and label-efficient — [PanDerm [B]](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12353815/); [Derm Foundation card [E]](https://huggingface.co/google/derm-foundation).

### Inferences
**Implementation sketch (this report's inference)**
1. **Embedding extraction.** Use the `transformers` `AutoModel` for `facebook/dinov2-small`, already in use, and `-base`. Store:
   - the CLS token;
   - the patch-token mean;
   - optionally, the patch mean inside the confirmed lesion box, or a crop embedding from the box enlarged by about 1.5×.

   Cache the vectors in SQLite or `.npy`, keyed by `image_id`, `model_id` and `preprocess_hash`.
2. **Normalization.** Within each fold, subtract the training-fold mean (SimpleShot "CL2N"), then L2-normalize.
3. **Classifiers.**
   - Cosine kNN with k ∈ {1, 3, 5, 7}, distance-weighted, using `sklearn.neighbors.NearestNeighbors(metric="cosine")`. With 120 vectors FAISS is unnecessary; `IndexFlatIP` on normalized vectors is the equivalent if the index grows.
   - Nearest-class-mean prototypes, with an option for per-patient-averaged prototypes so that patients with many photos do not dominate.
   - The existing logistic regression as the comparator.
4. **Patient-level exclusion.**
   - At training time, use StratifiedGroupKFold with groups = `patient_id`.
   - At query time, filter out neighbors with the same `patient_id`.
   - Also deduplicate near-identical photos, for example the same day and same hand.
5. **Evaluation.**
   - Balanced accuracy, normal-photo false-alarm rate, macro-F1 and the confusion matrix, with Wilson and bootstrap CIs. Use the same folds as EfficientNet and logistic regression.
   - Report the k-sensitivity curve.
   - With 9 normal photos, k > 3 rarely yields a normal majority. Use class-balanced voting or prototypes, and report it.
6. **UI (FastAPI + React).** A "similar reference cases" panel:
   - top-k per class, excluding the same patient;
   - each case shows its similarity score, its human-confirmed label and its source;
   - a research-only disclaimer;
   - no auto-write of labels. A user's choice is saved only on explicit confirmation, which keeps the project rules.
7. **GPU estimate.**
   - DINOv2-small or base inference on 120–1,000 images is trivial on any 8–16 GB GPU, and feasible on CPU, because of the model sizes (21M and 86M parameters). No source gives measured VRAM or time for this setting, so this is inference.
   - kNN and prototype classifiers are CPU-only and take milliseconds.

**NOW (~120 photos / 14 patients)**
- Expected benefit: comparable to logistic regression in accuracy; the expected gain is interpretability. kNN with patient exclusion gives an honest view of how cases cluster across patients.
- Applies: binary yes/no and 3-stage tasks, as an additional baseline and a viewer.
- Does not apply: claims of clinical similarity, or any metric-learning training (Siamese or triplet).
- Minimum data: kNN works with any n, but CIs will be very wide with 9 normal photos from few patients. 未找到可靠證據 for a minimum n for phlebitis.
- Risks: same-patient leakage, an over-trusted viewer, and privacy of displayed photos (see B2).

**FUTURE (~1,000 photos, three balanced classes)**
- Add a comparison of DINOv2-base or -large against a dermatology foundation model. Only proceed after the weights are converted to safetensors, the license is reviewed (PanDerm is CC-BY-NC-ND), and HAI-DEF terms are accepted by the user.
- Add crop embeddings from human-confirmed boxes.
- Consider partial fine-tuning (last blocks) or a linear probe. BSCD-FSL and Chen 2019 suggest fine-tuning wins once labelled data grows.
- FAISS is still optional at 1,000 vectors, since sklearn is enough.
- Retrieval quality can be measured as precision@k of the same stage from different patients.

### Gaps
- No measured VRAM or latency for DINOv2 inference on consumer GPUs from an official source (未找到可靠證據; the estimate above is inference from model size).
- FAISS Windows installation specifics were not verified (INSTALL.md not fetched).
